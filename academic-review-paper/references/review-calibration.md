# Review Calibration

Use this reference to calibrate the report to its audience while keeping the review fair, prioritized, and useful.

## Contents

- Purpose and audience
- Prioritization and confidence
- Constructive recommendations
- Adversarial review
- Literature and novelty

## Purpose And Audience

### Author Review

Help the author decide what must change before submission. Lead with a fair account of the contribution, then concentrate on the smallest set of issues that materially affect credibility, interpretation, or contribution. End with an ordered revision roadmap.

### Discussant Review

Help an audience understand the paper and help the author improve it. Convert the most important findings into constructive discussion points and questions. Do not simulate an editor, assign a journal verdict, or turn the report into line editing. Presentation takeaways should frame an oral discussion; they are not instructions to build slides.

### Journal Referee

Write for the authors unless the user requests a separate confidential section. Preserve anonymity and do not include local paths, the skill name, or implementation metadata. A recommendation or score belongs only in the confidential section or journal form when the confirmed assignment calls for it.

## Prioritization And Confidence

Use severity to express consequences, not tone:

- **Critical**: the central claim, identification, proof, model solution, or quantitative conclusion cannot currently be sustained.
- **Major**: the issue materially weakens the contribution but has a plausible revision path.
- **Minor**: a localized issue worth correcting that does not threaten the central contribution.
- **Observation**: a useful alternative framing or extension, not a defect.

Reserve critical findings for claims verified against the manuscript. Use confidence to expose evidentiary limits:

- **High**: directly established by the reviewed source.
- **Medium**: strongly supported, but a missing derivation, code path, data detail, or source could change the assessment.
- **Low**: plausible concern that requires information unavailable in the reviewed artifact.

Do not pad the report to reach a fixed number of findings. Combine concerns that share one underlying problem, and separate them only when they require different decisions or evidence.

## Constructive Recommendations

For each material concern, explain why it matters before suggesting a response. Recommend additional analysis, data, modeling, or rewriting only when it could actually discriminate between interpretations or repair the problem.

When the needed data or analysis may not be feasible, offer alternatives such as narrowing the claim, documenting the limitation, reframing the contribution, or identifying the design decision the author must make. Do not invent a convenient fix for a genuinely fatal or unresolved issue.

Acknowledge strong execution and intellectual contribution specifically. Presentational choices can be offered as options; identification, logical consistency, and source accuracy should be stated directly.

## Adversarial Review

An adversarial pass should pressure-test the balanced review, not duplicate it. Examine:

- the strongest coherent case against the central conclusion;
- breaks between assumptions or identifying variation, results, and interpretation;
- selective evidence, targets, specifications, model variants, or counterfactuals;
- alternative explanations or observationally equivalent mechanisms;
- overgeneralization beyond the sample, environment, calibration, or model support;
- whether the paper's contribution would remain if its preferred mechanism or model were removed.

Treat hypothetical vulnerabilities as such. In a full review, say whether each new adversarial point changes the prioritized findings or remains speculative.

## Literature And Novelty

Separate three claims:

1. what the manuscript says prior work finds;
2. what a cited source actually establishes when independently inspected;
3. the reviewer's inference about novelty or positioning.

Without external verification, use attribution such as "the manuscript characterizes" and mark suggested references for verification. Do not declare a paper first, missing, or incorrectly characterized solely from memory.
