# Theory And Quantitative Review Criteria

Use for formal theory, mechanism models, calibrated or estimated quantitative models, structural work, and mixed empirical-model papers. Apply only the sections relevant to the model's role.

## Contents

- Model purpose and mechanism
- Assumptions and solution
- Quantitative discipline
- Validation
- Counterfactuals and welfare
- Empirics-model bridge

## Model Purpose And Mechanism

Identify what conclusion requires the model. Ask whether a verbal mechanism, accounting decomposition, reduced-form exercise, or simpler model could deliver the same insight. Complexity is justified when it clarifies equilibrium forces, disciplines magnitudes, supports welfare analysis, or enables credible counterfactuals unavailable from the evidence alone.

Trace the core mechanism from primitives and equilibrium conditions to propositions, comparative statics, quantitative outcomes, and interpretation. Flag missing logical steps or mechanisms that the model assumes rather than explains.

## Assumptions And Solution

Separate assumptions that generate the result from those used mainly for tractability. For material assumptions, assess:

- economic plausibility in the stated environment;
- consistency with institutional facts or evidence offered by the paper;
- whether relaxing the assumption changes existence, sign, magnitude, welfare, or policy ranking;
- whether alternative mechanisms could generate the same prediction;
- whether definitions, timing, equilibrium objects, proofs, and solution concepts are internally consistent.

Verify alleged proof or algebra errors against the displayed derivation. If an appendix, code, or omitted step is needed, label the concern provisional.

## Quantitative Discipline

For calibrated, estimated, simulated, or structural models, map parameters to targets, likelihood variation, auxiliary estimates, or external evidence. Ask:

- Which moments or variation identify each important parameter or combination of parameters?
- Are targets chosen independently of the main result, or do they mechanically reproduce it?
- Are externally fixed parameters justified and sensitivity-tested?
- Are numerical solution, simulation, estimation, and standard-error procedures described well enough to assess?
- Are weak identification, local minima, normalization, or observational equivalence relevant?

Do not equate fit with identification.

## Validation

Distinguish targeted fit from validation. Evaluate untargeted moments, out-of-sample evidence, cross-sectional heterogeneity, event timing, transition dynamics, or external evidence when those comparisons bear on the model's intended use.

Check whether simulated mechanisms match empirical facts in sign, magnitude, timing, and heterogeneity. A good aggregate fit can conceal offsetting micro-level failures; a poor untargeted fit matters most when it is tied to the mechanism or counterfactual.

## Counterfactuals And Welfare

Assess whether counterfactuals remain within a credible region disciplined by data and model structure. Identify which assumptions drive extrapolation. When relevant, examine transition dynamics, distributional effects, fiscal closure, market clearing, political economy, and general-equilibrium feedback.

Welfare results should state whose welfare is measured, the baseline and horizon, aggregation or social weights, compensation assumptions, and the treatment of transition costs. Recommend an additional counterfactual or decomposition only when it clarifies the mechanism, policy incidence, or quantitative value added.

## Empirics-Model Bridge

For mixed papers, classify each empirical fact as motivation, parameter discipline, target, validation, or direct test. Ask whether model objects, parameters, wedges, shocks, and frictions map clearly to observed measures.

Check whether:

- multiple mechanisms can fit the same targeted moments;
- evidence that motivates the model also independently validates it;
- the model explains the paper's timing, heterogeneity, and magnitudes rather than sitting beside them;
- any empirical result contradicts the proposed mechanism;
- the quantitative exercise extends the evidence through welfare, dynamics, general equilibrium, or policy counterfactuals.

When the bridge is weak, identify the missing mapping, prediction, moment, or falsification exercise rather than asking generically for "more connection."
